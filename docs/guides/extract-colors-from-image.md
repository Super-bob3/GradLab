# How to Extract Colors from an Image

GradLab can pull a color palette directly from any photo, illustration, or screenshot. The extracted colors feed straight into the gradient engine, so you can create a gradient that matches your brand, artwork, or reference image.

## Step 1: Upload your image

In the panel, find the **Upload Image** area. Either:

- Click it to open a file picker
- Drag and drop an image file onto the upload zone

Supported formats: JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, and most other common image formats.

## Step 2: Sample colors

After uploading, the **Extract Colors** modal opens. It shows your image with colored markers placed automatically at diverse color points.

- **Drag a marker** to any point in the image to sample that color
- **Add markers** with the **+ Color** button to extract more colors
- **Remove markers** with the **− Color** button

The markers show a live preview of the sampled color. Move them around to find the exact tones you want.

## Step 3: Apply to the gradient

Click **Done**. The sampled colors replace the current palette in the gradient. The canvas updates immediately.

## Tips for great results

**Match a brand identity**
Upload a brand photo, campaign image, or screenshot of a product. Sample the dominant colors to create a gradient that's on-brand without manual color matching.

**Use complementary colors**
For the most visually pleasing gradients, place markers on areas with distinct hues rather than similar tones. A highlight, a midtone, and a shadow from the same image often produce natural-looking gradients.

**Reduce color count for cleaner gradients**
3–4 well-chosen colors usually produce cleaner results than 6–8 similar ones. Remove markers for any colors that feel redundant.

**Adjust after extraction**
The extracted colors are a starting point. After applying, you can click any swatch in the color list to fine-tune individual colors using the color editor.

## Uploading a background image

There is also a separate **Upload Background** option under Shape & Flow. This diffuses an image directly into the gradient rather than extracting discrete color stops — useful for blending photographic texture into the animation.
